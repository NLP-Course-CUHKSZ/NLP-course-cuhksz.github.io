    Why Efficiency? Scaling Laws, Costs and the Memory Gap

    Scaling laws reward ever more compute, model size grows far faster than GPU memory, and frontier models are expensive to use. The methods split into changes within the Transformer and changes that work on any trained network — and for LLMs, memory and communication join FLOPs as the costs that matter.

  4. 2

    Redundancy and Efficient Attention: Sparse, GQA and MLA

    Trained weights are low-rank and attention heads and FFN neurons are decomposable, so there is redundancy to remove. Sparse patterns (Longformer, Big Bird) let each token attend to fewer tokens; MQA, GQA and MLA store fewer keys and values per token.

    → Attention is cheaper — what about the FFN, which holds most of the parameters?

  5. 3

    Mixture of Experts: Routing, Experts and DeepSeekMoE

    A router sends each token to only a few expert FFNs: the capacity of a huge model at the compute of a small one. Top-k and hash routing, fine-grained and shared experts, load-balancing losses, DeepSeekMoE and DeepSeek-V2, and upcycling a dense model into an MoE.

    → What if the model is already trained, and simply too big?

  6. 4

    Quantization: Fewer Bits per Weight

    Snap every weight to a small integer grid with a scale and a zero point. Outliers stretch the grid, so LLM.int8() keeps them in FP16, and GPTQ and AWQ make 4-bit work. INT8 is almost free; INT4 is the sweet spot for local models.

    → We kept every weight, just with fewer bits — do we need every weight at all?
    Scale & zero pointINT4 and the outlier problemLLM.int8()Mixed-precision trainingGPTQ, AWQ, GGUFWhich method when
  7. 5

    Pruning and Knowledge Distillation

    Pruning removes weights, heads or whole layers; only structured pruning makes dense hardware faster, and the model needs to recover afterwards, as in LLM-Shearing. Distillation trains a small student on a teacher's soft labels — and for LLMs, mostly on the teacher's generated data.

    → The model is smaller — but how do we afford to train and adapt it?
    Prune and fine-tuneStructured vs. unstructuredLLM-ShearingSoft labelsTinyBERTData distillationSelf- and cross-model distillation
  8. IIITrain it cheaperTrain fewer parameters; split the rest across GPUs
  9. 6

    Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning: LoRA, Adapters, Modules

    Freeze the pre-trained model and learn a thin update: LoRA's B·A trains about 0.4% of a 4096×4096 matrix and merges back with zero latency. Adapters and prefix-tuning insert parameters elsewhere, and small modules might be composed like Lego bricks.

    → What if the model itself does not fit on one GPU?
    Where to insert parametersLoRALoRA in numbersAdaptersPrefix-tuningModular retrieval
  10. 7

    Distributed Training: Data and Model Parallelism, ZeRO

    Data parallelism copies the model and splits the data; model parallelism splits the model. Mixed-precision Adam needs 16 bytes per parameter — 112 GB for a 7B model — and ZeRO shards optimizer states, gradients and weights so memory per GPU falls with the number of GPUs.

    → Training happens once — inference runs billions of times. What makes it slow?
    Why distributedData parallelismModel parallelismDP vs. MP16 bytes per parameterVRAM for a 7B modelZeRO stages

  12. 8

    Inference Bottlenecks, the KV Cache and PagedAttention

    Prefill is compute-bound, decoding is memory-bound. The KV cache grows with every token and, not the weights, limits the batch size; PagedAttention stores it in small blocks like virtual memory, cutting the waste from 60–80% to under 4%.

    → Memory is under control — but each request still makes one big pass per token.
    Prefill vs. decodeWhy serving is hardWhat is the KV cache?KV bytes per tokenShrinking the cachePagedAttention
  13. 9

    Speculative Decoding: Draft, Verify, Stay Exact

    A small draft model proposes several tokens and the large model checks them all in one pass. Accept with probability min(1, q/p), otherwise resample from the normalized max(0, q−p): the output distribution is exactly the big model's, typically 2–3× faster.

    → Fewer big passes — but where does the time go inside one pass?
    Easy vs. hard tokensParallel verificationWorked exampleFaster and exactThe corrected distributionMeasured speedups
  14. 10

    FlashAttention: Tiling and the Online Softmax

    Standard attention writes the N×N score matrix to slow HBM and reads it back. FlashAttention computes attention tile by tile in on-chip SRAM, using a running max and sum to merge softmax blocks exactly: more FLOPs, 9× fewer bytes moved, 5.7× faster.

    → We have all the building blocks — how does a real serving engine combine them?
    The problem is memory trafficThe idea in one slideTilingOnline softmaxIO, not FLOPsAscend chips
  15. 11

    Serving with vLLM and the Road Ahead

    vLLM looks like an operating system: a scheduler with continuous batching, a block manager with page tables, copy-on-write sharing for parallel sampling and beam search, and preemption. The outlook: sparse and hybrid attention, low-bit formats, disaggregated serving and efficient reasoning.

4What's new in 2026

The lecture's outlook (slide 165) asks where the next ten-times saving will come from. Its answer: the frontier is co-design — model architecture, numerics, kernels and serving systems built together. Six trends to watch:

🔍

Sparse attention in production

DeepSeek-V3.2 adds a lightweight “lightning indexer” that scores past tokens and lets each query attend only to the top-k of them (k = 2,048) — Longformer's idea (Chapter 2), but learned.

DeepSeek-V3.2 technical report, arXiv:2512.02556 (Dec 2025) · slide 165
🔗

Hybrid linear attention

Most layers become linear-attention layers with a fixed-size state; only every fourth layer keeps a growing KV cache. Kimi Linear reports up to 75% less KV cache and up to 6× decoding throughput at 1M tokens.

Qwen3-Next (Gated DeltaNet, Sep 2025) · Kimi Linear, arXiv:2510.26692 (Oct 2025) · Nemotron-H · slide 165
🔢

Low-bit everything

FP8 training is now standard — DeepSeek-V3 was trained largely in FP8 (slide 55). NVFP4 on NVIDIA Blackwell stores 4-bit floats with one scale per block of 16 values: the group-wise quantization of Notebook 2, in hardware.

NVIDIA, Introducing NVFP4 (2025) · slide 165 (vendor cost claims unverified)
🚛

Disaggregated serving

Compute-bound prefill and memory-bound decode (slide 114) now run on separate GPU pools, with the KV cache moved between them over fast networks. Mooncake serves Kimi this way; llm-d brings it to Kubernetes on top of vLLM.

Mooncake, arXiv:2407.00079 (FAST '25) · llm-d (Red Hat and partners, May 2025)
🧠

Efficient reasoning

For reasoning models, thinking tokens dominate the bill. Thinking budgets, adaptive effort, multi-token prediction and speculative decoding (Chapter 9) all aim at cheaper long chains of thought.

Lecture outlook, slide 165 · DeepSeek-V3 multi-token prediction as a drafter, slide 126
🧱

Stack the tricks

The techniques are complementary. QLoRA trains LoRA adapters on a 4-bit base model, LongLoRA adds sparse attention to extend context cheaply, QMoE compresses trillion-parameter MoEs to under one bit per parameter.

Slide 166 · QLoRA (2023)

5Quotes & reading

"We should forget about small efficiencies, say about 97% of the time: premature optimization is the root of all evil. Yet we should not pass up our opportunities in that critical 3%."

Donald Knuth, Structured Programming with go to Statements, ACM Computing Surveys (1974)
The lecture's method: first ask where the time and memory go, then optimize the critical part.

"The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin."

Rich Sutton, The Bitter Lesson (2019)
If quality is bought with compute (slide 6), every saving buys a better model.

"We argue that a missing principle is making attention algorithms IO-aware — accounting for reads and writes between levels of GPU memory."

Tri Dao et al., FlashAttention (NeurIPS 2022)
Bytes moved, not FLOPs — the lesson of Chapter 10.

"Many insects have a larval form that is optimized for extracting energy and nutrients from the environment and a completely different adult form that is optimized for the very different requirements of traveling and reproduction."

Geoffrey Hinton, Oriol Vinyals and Jeff Dean, Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network (2015)
Train big, deploy small: the idea behind distillation and compression (Chapters 4–5).
