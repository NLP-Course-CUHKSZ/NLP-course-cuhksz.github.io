    Language Models in a Nutshell, and Why They Matter

    A language model scores how likely a text is, or equivalently predicts the next word; a neural LM generates "我思故我在" one token at a time. Because the text is its own label, the task scales, and filling blanks forces the model to learn arithmetic, sentiment, coreference and world knowledge.

    Sparsity, Smoothing and Evaluation: Perplexity and WER

    Large n copies the corpus, and 99.96% of possible Shakespeare bigrams never occur, so MLE gives most sentences probability zero. Smoothing moves mass to unseen events; perplexity (computed in log space) and word error rate tell us which model is better.

4What's new in 2026

The lecture ends with the architecture trade-offs (slide 143) and a September 2026 release (slide 144). Six things to watch around this lecture's topic:

➡️

Decoder-only is still the default

One simple objective on raw text, one text-in/text-out interface, cheap generation with the KV cache and predictable scaling: the deck's four reasons why almost all large models are decoder-only.

Slide 143 · slide 151
🔄

Encoder-decoder is back — causally

DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash splits 40 layers into a 20-layer causal encoder and a 20-layer decoder that reads one shared KV cache, about a quarter of V4-Flash's KV memory. The name is back; the bidirectional T5 recipe is not.

Slide 144 · DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash technical report (arXiv 2609.19969, Sep 2026)
📚

N-grams return, as memory

DeepSeek's Engram adds hashed n-gram embeddings that a Transformer looks up in constant time and gates into its hidden state. V4.1-Flash uses it with n-gram orders 2–4 and 196B Engram parameters next to its 552B backbone.

Engram (arXiv 2601.07372, Jan 2026) · arXiv 2609.19969
🌫

Not left to right: diffusion LMs

Diffusion language models write a whole block at once and refine it, a relative of BERT's masked-word prediction. DiffusionGemma, fine-tuned from Gemma 4, denoises 256-token blocks and can still decode autoregressively.

DiffusionGemma Technical Report (arXiv 2608.00146, Jul 2026)
🔍

Encoders still earn their keep

BERT-style models remain the tool for understanding, retrieval and embeddings. ModernBERT brought the encoder up to date: trained on 2 trillion tokens with a native 8,192-token context.

Slide 143 · ModernBERT (arXiv 2412.13663, Dec 2024)
🎲

A language model helped teach this lecture

The spin-the-wheel animation was built with Claude Opus 5.5 from the sampling slide, the Alice passage and a short instruction in Chinese — a model that itself writes by sampling the next token, just with a far better distribution.

5Quotes & reading

"You shall know a word by the company it keeps."

J. R. Firth, A Synopsis of Linguistic Theory 1930–1955 (1957), quoted on slide 94
Distributional semantics: the idea behind word2vec and every embedding since.

"The fundamental problem of communication is that of reproducing at one point either exactly or approximately a message selected at another point."

Claude Shannon, A Mathematical Theory of Communication (1948)
Text as a choice among possible messages — the probabilistic view of language. The same paper already generated English from word n-gram approximations, as in Chapter 5.

"But it must be recognized that the notion of ‘probability of a sentence’ is an entirely useless one, under any known interpretation of this term."

Noam Chomsky (1969), as quoted by Peter Norvig, On Chomsky and the Two Cultures of Statistical Learning
Norvig reads it as: a sentence never seen before must get probability zero. Smoothing (slide 66) and neural LMs are the answer.
