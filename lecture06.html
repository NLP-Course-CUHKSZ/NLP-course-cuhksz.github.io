Lecture 6: RL for LLM Post-Training — From Alignment to Reasoning

How did a text predictor become a helpful assistant, then a model that checks its own proofs, and now an agent? One tool did most of the work: reinforcement learning, fed by rewards from people, then from verifiers, then from the world. Watch the lecture in ten chapters with English and Chinese subtitles, follow the lecture map below, then try the ideas in Colab.

1The lecture map

Why does reinforcement learning, not more supervised fine-tuning, align LLMs and teach them to reason?

Four acts, ten chapters. Imitation gives a model the format of an assistant; RL on human preferences aligns it. Opening the RL toolbox shows that PPO, ReMax and GRPO differ mainly in their baseline. With rewards that are checked instead of learned, the same tool produces reasoning models, and next it has to teach models to act. The algorithm barely changes; the source of the reward does.   1. IAlignFrom imitation to human preferences
  2. 1

    How LLMs Are Trained: Pre-Training, Mid-Training and SFT

    Pre-training gives knowledge, mid-training sharpens what post-training will unlock, and SFT teaches the format of an assistant. But SFT sees only positive examples, copies its demonstrator, and optimizes likelihood rather than what people want.

    → Humans can compare answers more easily than write them — how do we train on comparisons?
    Three training stagesMid-trainingPre-training vs. SFTBack-translationWhat SFT cannot do
  3. 2

    Alignment via RLHF: From Summaries to ChatGPT

    Helpful, honest and harmless cannot be written as a loss. RLHF learns a reward model from pairwise comparisons with the Bradley–Terry model, then optimizes the policy with PPO and a KL leash. InstructGPT and ChatGPT scaled this recipe up.

    → Can we learn from the same preference pairs without a reward model and PPO?
    The three H'sOver-refusalPolicy gradientBradley–TerryRLHF in three stepsRLHF beats SFTInstructGPT
  4. 3

    DPO, Biased Judges and Reward Hacking

    DPO turns the RLHF objective into a single classification-style loss on preference pairs. But preference labels are biased (fake references fool every judge), and RL exploits a learned reward: Goodhart's law.

    → What if the reward is checked instead of learned — and which RL algorithm should optimize it?
    DPOOn- vs. off-policyMeasuring judge biasWho is biased?Reward hacking
  5. IIThe RL toolboxCheaper baselines, checkable rewards
  6. 4

    RL for LLMs: PPO, REINFORCE and ReMax

    Text generation is an RL problem with heterogeneous rewards and enormous compute. PPO's critic doubles memory; since LLM feedback is fast and deterministic, REINFORCE with a greedy-answer baseline (ReMax) suffices.

    → If sampling gets cheap, can a whole group of samples give a better baseline?
    Text as an RL problemHeterogeneous rewardsPPOThe price of PPOGeneral RL vs. RL for LLMsReMaxAlgorithms drive infrastructure
  7. 5

    GRPO, RLVR and the Frontier of RL for LLMs

    GRPO uses the group mean as baseline, and its variants (Dr. GRPO, DAPO, GSPO, …) fix stability at scale. Verifiable rewards replace learned ones (RLVR); the frontier moves to asynchronous systems and to exploring hard prompts.

    → What happens when this tool, fed by verifiers, is applied at scale?
    ReMax → GRPOGRPOThe GRPO familyMap of algorithmsSFT memorizes, RL generalizesRLHF vs. RLVRNo gradient on hard promptsKnapsack RLIncentivize, don't teach

  9. 6

    Large Reasoning Models: OpenAI o1 and o3

    Long chains of thought outline, plan, backtrack and self-evaluate. o1 improves with both train-time RL and test-time thinking; reasoning tokens make thinking cost real money; o3 thinks with images.

    → How do we measure reasoning fairly?
    Benchmarks shoot upLong CoTo1's thinking habitsTrain- and test-time computeReasoning tokensHacking reasoning tokensThinking with images
  10. 7

    Measuring Reasoning and Inference-Time Verification

    Math, code, GPQA and Humanity's Last Exam are checkable and unsaturated. One path to reasoning: sample many solutions and verify them, guided step by step by a value model (OVM) or a process reward model.

    → Learned verifiers can be hacked — is there a simpler path?
    Why math, code, GPQAGPQAHumanity's Last ExamRepeated samplingOVM decodingProcess vs. outcomeTheorem proving
  11. 8

    Long-CoT Distillation, DeepSeek-R1 and SimpleRL

    Distilling long chains cannot beat the teacher. DeepSeek-R1-Zero showed that pure RL with rule-based rewards makes long reasoning and reflection emerge; SimpleRL reproduced the core result on a 7B model with 8K examples.

    → A right final answer is not a right proof — how do we reward rigorous reasoning?
    Synthesizing long CoTLength emerges in RLThe aha momentR1 pipelineSimpleRL recipeRL vs. SFT on MATHTraining phasesHypotheses
  12. 9

    Self-Verification, Olympiad Gold and ARC-AGI

    DeepSeekMath-V2 trains a proof verifier, checked by a meta-verifier, and rewards the generator with it, reaching open IMO gold. ARC-AGI asks a different question: can a model learn a new skill, or a new game, from a few tries?

    → What about domains with no answer key, and models that must act?

4What's new in 2026

The lecture's later chapters track what changed this year. Six trends to watch, each with the slide that covers it:

🥇

Olympiad gold in small open models

DeepSeekMath-V2 was the first open model at IMO gold level. NVIDIA's Nemotron-Cascade 2, an open 30B MoE with 3B active parameters, reports gold-level results at IMO 2025, IOI and the ICPC World Finals, with 20× fewer parameters.

Slide 161 · slide 162 · arXiv:2603.19220 (Mar 2026)
🧩

ARC-AGI-2 fell in eight months

From 24% (the Kaggle winner, Nov 2025) to 54% (GPT-5.2 Pro, Feb 2026) to over 90% for frontier models at maximum reasoning effort (Jul 2026), past the 85% bar. Cost per task is now the open question.

Slide 165 · ARC Prize verified results
🎮

ARC-AGI-3: act, don't answer

135 interactive games with no instructions, scored on action efficiency against humans. Frontier models scored under 1% at launch (Mar 2026) and about 30% verified by July. The harness matters as much as the model.

Slide 166 · arcprize.org
⏳

Reasoning is bought with tokens, and now metered

Some 2026 models spend most of their output on reasoning. Frontier APIs hide raw chains of thought, and leaderboards report cost and tokens per task, so cheap catch-up by copying traces is getting harder.

Slide 98 · slide 99
⚙️

Agentic RL is a systems problem

Rollouts with hundreds of tool calls and long tails push training to asynchronous pipelines, thousands of sandboxes, and fixes for train–inference mismatch. The focus moves from reasoning thinking to agentic thinking.

Slide 74 · slide 175 · slide 177
🩺

Domain adaptation with RL alone

OnePO adapts a base model to medicine in one stage of RL with a “retiring” teacher: 67.2 on HealthBench versus 64.5 for SFT + RL on the same 20K samples. The released HuatuoGPT-3-27B reaches 70.1.

Slide 172 · slide 173 (our group's work)

5Quotes & reading

"When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

Goodhart's law, in Marilyn Strathern's wording (1997), quoted on slide 37
Why a learned reward model gets hacked once RL optimizes against it.

"Our large-scale reinforcement learning algorithm teaches the model how to think productively using its chain of thought in a highly data-efficient training process."

OpenAI, Learning to Reason with LLMs (2024), on slide 100
The o1 recipe in one sentence: RL, chain of thought, data efficiency.

"Rather than explicitly teaching the model on how to solve a problem, we simply provide it with the right incentives, and it autonomously develops advanced problem-solving strategies."

DeepSeek-AI, DeepSeek-R1 (2025), on the “aha moment”
“Incentivize, don't teach” (slide 80), in the authors' own words.

"The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin."

Rich Sutton, The Bitter Lesson (2019)
Why RLVR, whose signal scales with compute rather than labelling, took over.
