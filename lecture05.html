    What's New, a Transformer Recap and Why Scale Matters

    The field moves in weeks: new models, film-quality video and an agent that “actually does things”. Back to the Transformer: less built-in structure scales better, loss falls along a power law, and some abilities only switch on at scale.

    What Is an LLM? Compression, AGI and How Large Is Large

    One model for nearly everything. Predicting the next word well forces a network to compress the internet — and the facts in it. “Large” means billions of parameters, enormous compute and trillions of tokens (36 trillion for Qwen3).

    → Why is all that size worth it?
    One model for everythingThe one-minute ruleIntelligence as compressionNext word ⇒ world knowledgeParametersTrillions of tokens

    Why Larger Models: Emergent Abilities and Generalization

    Bigger models know more, learn tasks from a few examples or a plain description, and generalize by recombining what they have seen. Even techniques such as chain-of-thought and RLHF only start to help above a certain size.

    → How did these ingredients come together in practice?

  10. 6

    Training from Zero: The Pipeline and Tokenization (BPE)

    A model learns like a student: words, textbooks, exercises, a teacher's feedback. Step zero is tokenization: subwords balance vocabulary size and sequence length, BPE learns them by merging frequent pairs, and a tokenizer that ignores your language makes everything slower and costlier.

    → With tokens in hand, how does the model read its textbooks?
    From zero to ChatGPTThe student analogySubword modelingBPELLaMA on ChineseTokens beyond text
  11. 7

    Pretraining: Language Modeling and Eight Data Tips

    Pretraining is next-token prediction over trillions of tokens, and the secret sauce is the data: sources and mixing ratios, continued pretraining, and eight tips — filter, reformat, deduplicate, mix, order, scale (Chinchilla), mask and synthesize.

    → A base model continues text — how do we make it follow instructions?
    Language modelingMixing ratiosContinued pretrainingDeduplicationChinchillaSynthetic data
  12. 8

    Instruction Finetuning (SFT): Hypotheses and Tips

    Language modeling is not assisting users. SFT trains on instruction–response pairs; it mostly teaches format (LIMA), sometimes knowledge, and generalizes with diverse tasks (FLAN). Tips: distillation, self-instruct, loss on the output only, complex instructions.

    → The token-level loss treats every mistake alike — can we optimize directly for what humans prefer?
    LM ≠ assisting usersSuperficial alignmentFLAN-T5Alpaca / self-instructLoss on output onlyLimitations
  13. 9

    RLHF: Reward Models, InstructGPT and ChatGPT

    Comparing answers is easier than writing them. Learn a reward model from human comparisons, then optimize the policy against it with PPO. A 1.3B RLHF model beat a 12.9B supervised one; InstructGPT scaled the recipe and ChatGPT added conversation — but human judges have biases.

    → Where do the rest of us get conversation data — and how do the stages compare?
    Helpful, honest, harmlessReward modelPairwise comparisonsThe full pipelineRLHF beats 10× sizeJudgement biases

  15. 10

    Multi-turn Data, PlatoLM and Pretraining vs. SFT vs. RL

    ShareGPT gave open models real conversations; PlatoLM trains a user simulator to ask like people do. Pretraining and SFT share one loss, so documents can be turned into Q&A. RL generalizes where SFT memorizes: incentivize, don't teach.

    → If data and RL matter so much, do we still need ever-larger models?

    Small LLMs, the Densing Law and the End of Scaling?

    Data and hardware put walls in front of scaling. Small models on phones, Phi-4 built on synthetic data, and capability density that keeps rising. Pretraining as we know it may end — next come test-time scaling, agents, denser models and applications.

4What's new in 2026

The lecture opens with the news of early 2026 (slide 2) and closes Chapter 4 with three trends of the modern LLM (slide 59). Six things to watch:

⏱

“State of the art” lasts weeks

Around the 2026 Spring Festival almost every major Chinese lab shipped a new model, ByteDance released a new video model, and OpenAI followed with GPT-5.4 — which is why the course teaches principles, not model names.

Qwen 3.5-Plus · GLM-5 · MiniMax M2.5 · Kimi K2.5 · Seedance 2.0 · GPT-5.4 (slide 2)
🦞

Agents that actually do things

OpenClaw runs on your own machine, talks to you through your chat apps, remembers, browses and runs commands — and became popular far beyond developers. The lecture's homework: install it and find where it fails.

OpenClaw (slides 5–6, 68–69) · Lecture 7 covers agents and harness engineering
🎯

RL becomes a main training stage

After o1 and DeepSeek-R1, reinforcement learning is no longer a final polish: it is a major, compute-heavy stage, especially for reasoning and agentic tasks.

Slide 59 · DeepSeek-R1 (2025) · Lecture 6
🔁

Depth by looping, small models by on-policy distillation

Looped Transformers reuse one block several times for more computation per token without more parameters. On-policy distillation lets a teacher grade the student's own answers token by token.

Slide 59 · Thinking Machines Lab, On-Policy Distillation (Oct 2025)
🏭

Models build the data for the next models

Qwen3 was pretrained on 36 trillion tokens in 119 languages, with text extracted from PDFs and trillions of synthesized tokens; 40% of Phi-4's pretraining comes from synthetic data.

Qwen3 Technical Report (slide 28) · Phi-4 (slide 104)
💎

Denser, not just bigger

With data running out and servers limited in memory, the focus shifts from the scaling law to capability density: the same ability with fewer parameters. The densing-law paper finds that the maximum density of open models has doubled roughly every three months.

Densing Law of LLMs (slide 164) · slide 161

5Quotes & reading

"The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin."

Rich Sutton, The Bitter Lesson (2019)
The idea behind slide 9: less hand-built structure, more scale.

"The dialogue format makes it possible for ChatGPT to answer followup questions, admit its mistakes, challenge incorrect premises, and reject inappropriate requests."

OpenAI, Introducing ChatGPT (2022), quoted on slide 45
ChatGPT's breakthrough was in interaction and alignment, not in raw knowledge.

"Almost all knowledge in large language models is learned during pretraining, and only limited instruction tuning data is necessary to teach models to produce high quality output."

Zhou et al., LIMA: Less Is More for Alignment (2023)
The superficial alignment hypothesis of slide 112 — with its question mark.

"Pre-training as we know it will end. … Data is not growing: we have but one internet, the fossil fuel of AI."

Ilya Sutskever, NeurIPS 2024 talk, shown on slide 166
Why the lecture ends with denser models, reasoning and agents instead of “just scale”.
