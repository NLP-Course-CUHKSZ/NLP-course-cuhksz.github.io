Lecture 7: LLM Agents and Harness Engineering

How do you turn a good model into something that actually gets work done? Put it in a loop, give it planning, tools, memory and actions, and engineer everything around it — the harness. 4What's new in 2026

The lecture's outlook (slide 132) lists where harness engineering is going. Six trends to watch:

🔧

The harness got a name

"Harness engineering" became the 2026 term for building the system a model runs in. In OpenAI's case study, Codex agents wrote a whole internal product — about 1,500 merged pull requests and roughly a million lines in five months — while engineers designed the agents' workplace (slide 95).

OpenAI, Harness engineering (Feb. 11, 2026)
📊

A score belongs to a model–harness pair

On Terminal-Bench, the same model scores 10–20 points apart in different harnesses, more than the gap between many frontier models. Leaderboards now list every model–harness pair as its own row (slide 81, slide 107).

Terminal-Bench 2.0 / 2.1 leaderboards, as shown on the slides; numbers change with each release
🔗

One open protocol stack

MCP for tools, A2A between agents, AGENTS.md and skills for know-how. MCP, AGENTS.md and goose moved to a neutral foundation, and Agent Skills became an open standard (slide 109, slide 110).

Linux Foundation: Agentic AI Foundation (Dec. 9, 2025) · agentskills.io (Dec. 18, 2025)
📱

Personal agents that never sleep

The race for the personal agent: a real-time front end in front of an agent harness, as in MOSS-Live, or an always-on gateway with a heartbeat that wakes the agent to check its to-do list, as in OpenClaw (slide 2, slide 3, slide 102).

MOSS-Live (Nov. 2026) · OpenClaw
🧠

Models absorb the scaffolding

"Let's think step by step" became reasoning models, ReAct templates became native tool calling, and hand-written compaction became models trained to compact their own context. What stays in the harness: permissions, state, connectors and evals (slide 128, slide 129).

Sutton, The Bitter Lesson (2019), applied to harnesses (Chapter 8)
🎯

Benchmarks measure finishing the job

From "answer the question" to "finish the job": ARC-AGI-3 asks agents to learn unknown games by playing them; at launch humans solved every environment and frontier models scored under 1% (slide 147). RSI-Exam replays an agent's research artifact on a sealed split (slide 149).

ARC Prize, ARC-AGI-3 technical report (launched Mar. 25, 2026)

5Quotes & reading

"What magical trick makes us intelligent? The trick is that there is no trick. The power of intelligence stems from our vast diversity, not from any single, perfect principle."

Marvin Minsky, The Society of Mind (1986), quoted on slide 21
Agents are not new — and the multi-agent societies of Chapter 10 are Minsky's question with LLMs as the agents.

"Consistently, the most successful implementations weren't using complex frameworks or specialized libraries. Instead, they were building with simple, composable patterns."

Anthropic, Building Effective Agents (Dec. 2024)
Why Claude Code is a deliberately simple single loop (Chapter 7), and principle 1: start with one loop.

"Good context engineering means finding the smallest possible set of high-signal tokens that maximize the likelihood of some desired outcome."

Anthropic, Effective Context Engineering for AI Agents (Sept. 2025)
The principle behind slide 83: context is a finite attention budget.

"The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin."

Rich Sutton, The Bitter Lesson (2019)
Why the best harness gets thinner as models get stronger — and principle 10: delete regularly.
