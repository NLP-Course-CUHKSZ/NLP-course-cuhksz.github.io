Your first week

The closing advice of the lecture (slide 98), plus the rules of thumb from the project page (slide 18):

  1. Form your team this week, and agree on who does what; the report needs a concrete contribution statement (slide 5).
  2. Skim the project page tonight and pick two candidate briefs, or write down your own LLM-related question (slide 16).
  3. Talk to a TA whose background fits your topic, with a concrete question (slides 67–68).
  4. Freeze the task, a held-out split and the primary metric, and have a working baseline by the end of week one.
  • One change — test one idea at a time.
  • Matched budget — your method and the baseline get the same compute, tokens or model calls.
  • Held-out split — never tune on your test data.
  • Independent checker — don't let the model grade its own work.
  • Seeds — run more than once.
  • Replayable traces — log everything so someone can check it.

4What's new in 2026

The Fall 2026 tracks add several things that were not there last year. Six to know before you pick a topic:

🧰

Skills are a project

An Agent Skill is a folder of instructions, sometimes with scripts, that an agent loads only when it needs it. New this year: build one for a real recurring task and show that it measurably helps (brief A9).

Lecture: slide 19, slide 21 · Track A
📝

AI can write a passable paper

A paper generated end to end by Sakana AI's AI Scientist-v2 passed peer review at an ICLR 2025 workshop (scores 6, 7, 6) — reporting a negative result — and was withdrawn after review. Use AI tools, describe how, and own every claim.

Sakana AI, Mar. 2025 · slide 81
📋

Rubrics for open-ended answers

When there is no single gold answer, experts write what a good answer must contain. HealthBench has 5,000 health conversations and 48,562 rubric criteria from 262 physicians; the same rubrics can become RL rewards (brief D8).

HealthBench (OpenAI, May 2025) · slide 39
⏱

Speedruns as a research lab

The community nanoGPT speedrun trains a 124M-parameter GPT-2-style model to 3.28 validation loss on FineWeb as fast as possible on 8×H100. Try one architecture change at matched time and plot the curve.

KellerJordan/modded-nanogpt · slide 23
🏥

Agents at the doctor's desk

OpenClaw-Med runs on a small local machine in the CUHK-Shenzhen hospital: it drafts a reference from the record, then suggests diagnosis and treatment once results are in. The doctor stays in control at every step.

Lecture: slide 61, slide 62 · want to deploy something? slide 63
🏆

Contests and startups count

Competitions (Track G) and startup prototypes (Track H) are formal project options — but the grade is the controlled comparison, and for startups retention and cost, not the leaderboard or the pitch deck.

Lecture: slide 45, slide 46 · Track G · Track H

5Quotes & reading

"If you do not work on an important problem, it's unlikely you'll do important work."

Richard Hamming, You and Your Research (Bell Communications Research colloquium, 1986)
Chapters 2–5: pick a question worth four weeks — and one you can actually attack.

"The first principle is that you must not fool yourself — and you are the easiest person to fool."

Richard Feynman, Cargo Cult Science, Caltech commencement address (1974)
Why the rules of thumb exist: matched budgets, held-out splits and independent checkers.

"Never use a long word where a short one will do."

George Orwell, Politics and the English Language (1946)
"Utilize" or "use"? Just say "use" (slide 89).

"一篇好的论文读起来，应该是像看一个流畅的电影，没有尿点。"

Benyou Wang, on slide 83
A good paper reads like a smooth film: no moment where the reader can safely leave.
