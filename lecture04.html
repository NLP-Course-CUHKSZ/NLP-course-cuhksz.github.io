1The lecture map

How should a neural network compose words into meaning — and why did the Transformer's answer win?

Five acts, eleven chapters. Every encoder needs two operations: composition (letting tokens exchange information) and abstraction (transforming the features of each token). MLPs, CNNs and RNNs mix the two and pay with fixed windows, local reach or long, fragile paths. The Transformer separates them — attention composes, feed-forward layers abstract — and it keeps improving as it scales. Click a chapter to watch it.

    Multilayer Perceptrons and Neural Language Models

    Concatenate word embeddings, apply fully-connected layers, learn from the prediction error by backpropagation — one worked gradient step lifts P(Positive) from 0.40 to 0.81. Bengio's neural LM uses the same recipe over a fixed window.

    → A fixed input layout cannot handle variable lengths — can we reuse one filter at every position?

  6. 4

    Convolutional Networks and ConvS2S

    Convolution multiplies a window by a filter and sums, sliding over 2D images or 1D token sequences. Causal convolutions generate text by predict-append-repeat; ConvS2S trains all positions in parallel with teacher forcing.

    → A window only sees its neighbours — can a state carry the whole past?
    Multiply, then sum2D vs. 1D convolutionAutoregressive generationConvS2S
  7. 5

    Recurrent Networks: RNN, LSTM and GRU

    An RNN reuses one update to carry a hidden state through time, so any length works — but steps run one after another. Teacher forcing trains on gold prefixes; LSTM gates keep, write and expose memory, and the GRU resets, then blends.

    → Can recurrence be made efficient again — and why do distant words still fade?

    Mamba, RWKV and Gradient Flow Through Time

    Transformer Variants, Applications and Ablations

    Attention is quadratic in length; Linformer compresses, Longformer and Big Bird sparsify. The same block runs on image patches, diffusion latents, robot actions and music. Ablations test what each part does: pure attention loses rank, MLP-Mixer and FNet mix tokens without attention.

4What's new in 2026

The lecture already points at the newest designs (Kimi Linear, slide 111; new scaling directions, slide 144). Six trends to watch:

🧬

Hybrid attention goes mainstream

Instead of full attention everywhere, several open models use cheap linear-attention layers (Gated DeltaNet, Kimi Delta Attention) for most of the stack and keep about one full-attention layer in four.

Kimi Linear (Oct 2025, slide 111) · Qwen3-Next (Sep 2025) · Qwen3.5 (Feb 2026)
🔄

Recurrence, reinvented

Mamba and RWKV keep a fixed-size state instead of a growing cache of keys and values, so inference work grows linearly with length — the recurrent idea of Chapter 5, revisited with learned, input-dependent updates.

Lecture slide 61 (Mamba) · slide 62 (RWKV)
🧭

Position moves into the layers

RoPE rotates queries and keys rather than adding a vector; Kimi Linear goes further and uses no explicit position encoding in its full-attention layers, letting the recurrent layers carry order.

Lecture slide 110 (RoPE) · slide 111 (NoPE in Kimi Linear)
✂

Sparse attention in production

DeepSeek Sparse Attention adds a small "lightning indexer" that picks which earlier tokens each query attends to — a learned version of the sparse patterns on slide 116, aimed at long contexts.

DeepSeek-V3.2-Exp (Sep 2025) · DeepSeek-V3.2 (arXiv:2512.02556) · also used in GLM-5
🪜

Attention across depth

Attention Residuals replace the fixed sum of a residual stream with softmax attention over earlier layers, so each layer chooses which earlier layers to read — attention over depth instead of over time.

Kimi Team, Attention Residuals (Mar 2026, arXiv:2603.15031) · mentioned on slide 111
⏳

Scaling beyond parameters

Compute spent at test time (sampling, longer reasoning, search), during RL training, on tools, memory and agents is now measured as its own axis — with gains that depend on how good the selection signal is.

Lecture slides 141–144 · OpenAI, Learning to Reason with LLMs (2024)

5Quotes & reading

"We propose a new simple network architecture, the Transformer, based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely."

Vaswani et al., Attention Is All You Need (2017)
The sentence that ended Act II — and the diagram of Chapter 7.

"The biggest lesson that can be read from 70 years of AI research is that general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin."

Richard Sutton, The Bitter Lesson (2019), quoted on slide 29
Why a weaker inductive bias can win once data and compute grow.

"The loss scales as a power-law with model size, dataset size, and the amount of compute used for training, with some trends spanning more than seven orders of magnitude."

Kaplan et al., Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (2020)
The empirical basis of Chapter 10.

"…emergent abilities appear due to the researcher's choice of metric rather than due to fundamental changes in model behavior with scale."

Schaeffer, Miranda & Koyejo, Are Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models a Mirage? (2023)
The counterpoint on slide 147: read capability curves as carefully as loss curves.
