1The lecture map

The whole lecture answers one question. Each part adds one ability, and each step exists because the previous one left something unsolved. Click a chip to jump straight to that slide in the video.

How do we give a language model eyes, ears, a voice and hands — and a sense of space?
  1. 1
    Why

    Text is a narrow window

    Humans perceive through many senses at once, the web is multimodal, and high-quality text is running out. So: can a model perceive the world the way we do?

    Part 1聽 = ear + eye + heartWhy multimodalityMilestones
  2. 2
    See · align

    Put images and text in one space: CLIP

    Contrastive learning on hundreds of millions of web image–caption pairs gives zero-shot recognition and text-to-image search. But CLIP can only match — it cannot talk about what it sees.

    Part 2CLIPContrastive objectiveZero-shot classificationRetrieval
  3. 3
    See · talk

    Let a language model read images: Flamingo

    Keep a frozen vision encoder and a frozen LLM, and connect them with a Perceiver Resampler and gated cross-attention, trained on interleaved web pages. Few-shot multimodal dialogue — the "GPT-3 moment" for vision.

    ArchitecturePerceiver ResamplerGated cross-attentionCLIP vs. Flamingo
  4. 4
    Build

    Language as the pivot: encoder + connector + LLM

    Either the LLM is a controller that calls expert tools, or vision is a joint part of the model (LLaVA, Qwen-VL), up to native models like GPT-4o and Gemini and models that also generate images. Which encoder, which connector, how many tokens per image?

    Part 3Language as pivotLLM as controllerLLaVAGPT-4o & GeminiVisual encoders
  5. 5
    Train

    Align first, then teach it to follow instructions

    Stage 1 aligns image features with the LLM on captions; stage 2 uses visual instruction data, often written with GPT's help. The data recipe matters as much as the architecture.

    Part 4Visual instruction tuningGPT-assisted dataTypical training paradigm
  6. 6
    Hear & speak

    Turn speech into tokens, then talk in real time

    Speech is long, continuous and rich in tone. Tokenize it (semantic vs. acoustic tokens), interleave it with text, listen while speaking (full duplex), and split the work between a thinker and a talker. Voice may become the ultimate interface.

    Part 5Why speech is hardSemantic vs. acoustic tokensInterleavingFull duplexThinker–Talker
  7. 7
    Imagine

    Generate images and videos — and simulate worlds

    Unify understanding and generation, predict the next frame, and use video models as world simulators. But is the generated world physically right?

    Part 6JanusNext-frame predictionMedical world simulatorMicro world simulator
  8. 8
    Act

    From seeing to acting: VLA and world models

    Vision-language-action models map pixels and instructions to motor commands; System 2 plans while System 1 acts; world-action models predict both the future and the action. Embodiment is where perception meets consequences.

    Part 7VLA modelsSystem 2 / System 1World modelsWorld-action models
  9. 9
    Space

    Understand and build the 3D world

    Images are only projections. Feed-forward 3D reconstruction, 3D generation, and an LLM that writes scene graphs. What should a model know about space that pictures can't tell it?

    Part 8Why 3D3D reconstructionLLM as scene programmer
  10. 10
    Outlook

    Open problems and what changed in 2026

    Efficiency, evaluation, more modalities, edge devices — and why multimodal LLMs matter for society.

    Part 9EfficiencyEvaluationEdge devices2026: what changed
The thread: every step widens what the model can perceive or do, while language stays the hub that connects the modalities — first matching images to text, then reading them, hearing and speaking, imagining, acting, and finally reasoning about space.

4Try it in Colab

Three short notebooks turn the lecture into experiments. Click Open in Colab, then Runtime → Run all. Each ends with exercises. Notebook 2 needs a free T4 GPU (Runtime → Change runtime type); the others also run on CPU.

Notebook 1 · Part 2 · ~5 min

CLIP: zero-shot classification and retrieval

  • Classify images into labels CLIP never trained on
  • See a prompt raise P(cat) from 0.24 to 0.69
  • Search images with text, and find where it fails
  • Compute the contrastive loss by hand
.ipynb · CPU or GPU
Notebook 2 · Parts 3–4 · ~10 min

Chat with an open multimodal LLM

  • Ask Qwen2.5-VL-3B about images, alone or several at once
  • Count how many tokens an image becomes
  • Find the vision encoder, connector and LLM inside the model
.ipynb · T4 GPU
Notebook 3 · Part 5 · ~5 min

Speech as tokens

  • Compress speech with a neural codec and listen at 1.5–12 kbps
  • Check with Whisper whether the words survive
  • See that speech needs ~160× more tokens than its transcript
.ipynb · CPU or GPU

5What's new in 2026

The lecture's last part (slide 127) lists what changed this year. Six trends to watch:

🎤

Omni is the default

Real-time speech-and-vision models are the new baseline, and assistants now listen while they speak and keep working in the background.

Qwen3.5-Omni (Apr 2026) · MOSS-Live full-duplex assistant (Nov 2026)
🌎

World models became products

Interactive world models moved from research demos to products for driving simulation and developers.

Genie 3 → Waymo World Model · World Labs' World API
🤖

From VLA to world-action models

Robot models now predict the future as well as the action, and can be steered by prompts instead of retraining.

DreamZero · GR00T N2 · Physical Intelligence π0.7 (Apr 2026)
🧠

General models go physical

Frontier general-purpose models are being evaluated as robot planners and released in robotics editions.

GPT-6 Astra as robot planner · Gemini Robotics 2
🧱

3D in one forward pass

Feed-forward reconstruction and native 3D generation are now standard tools rather than hours of per-scene optimization.

VGGT and successors (Part 8)
🔗

One embedding space for everything

Embedding models now map text, images, video, audio and PDFs into a single space — CLIP's idea, extended to every modality.

Gemini Embedding 2 (Mar 2026)
