    From Characters to Words: Scripts, Unicode, Tokens

    Chinese characters carry sound and meaning; encodings from GB2312 to Unicode and UTF-8 map them to bytes. Spaces do not define words, normalization is a trade-off, and Zipf's long tail guarantees out-of-vocabulary words.

    → How can a model represent a word it has never seen?

    Morphemes, Chinese Words and Byte Pair Encoding

    Words are built from morphemes; Chinese words have little inflection and ambiguous boundaries (乒乓球拍卖完了). GPT-style models sidestep both with byte-level BPE, which learns frequent pieces and can always fall back to bytes. Parts of speech give words a grammatical role.

    → Words have categories — how do they combine into sentences?

    Sentences and Discourse: Parse Trees to Coherence

    Phrase-structure trees group words into constituents; dependency trees link each word to its head. Ambiguity ("I shot an elephant in my pajamas") makes parsing hard, and discourse relations connect sentences into a coherent text.

    Words for Search: Term Matching, TF-IDF and LSA

    Search needs a score, not just a match. The vector space model with TF-IDF weights ranks documents; latent semantic analysis compresses the term-document matrix with SVD so related words meet even without exact matches.

    Distributional Semantics: Context Becomes Vectors

    Hand-built resources like WordNet are costly; the distributional hypothesis lets text do the work. You can guess "ongchoi" from its contexts, and so can a computer: co-occurrence counts, cosine similarity, PPMI weighting, and SVD for dense vectors.

    → Instead of counting and factorizing, can we learn dense vectors by prediction?

  13. 8

    Word Embeddings and the Word2vec Skip-Gram Model

    Embedding spaces capture neighbors, analogies (king − man + woman), cross-lingual structure, semantic change — and social bias. Skip-gram learns them by predicting context words with a softmax over the vocabulary, using two tables of 2|V|d parameters.

    → Every update sums over the whole vocabulary — how can training be cheap?

    Negative Sampling, GloVe and Evaluating Embeddings

    Negative sampling turns prediction into a few binary decisions, touching K + 2 vectors per update. CBOW flips the direction, GloVe fits global counts, pretrained vectors save training, and intrinsic and extrinsic tests answer different questions.

    → Where exactly does the full-softmax cost come from?

    Wrap-Up and Appendix: Deriving Word2vec Gradients

    A recap of the thread from structure to representation, then the calculus: the center-word gradient is the expected context vector minus the observed one, and the full-softmax update costs about |V|·d per pair, against K·d with negative sampling.

4What's new in 2026

Word2vec and GloVe date from 2013–2014, and BPE entered NLP in 2016. The questions in this lecture — what a token should be, and what a vector should capture — are still moving. Five developments to watch:

📚

Bigger, more multilingual vocabularies

GPT-2's byte-level BPE vocabulary (the idea on slide 61) has 50,257 tokens. Llama 3 moved to 128K tokens and reports up to 15% fewer tokens than Llama 2; Gemma 3 uses 262K, chosen to be more balanced for non-English text — the cost gap that slide 58 shows for Chinese.

Meta, Introducing Llama 3 (Apr 2024) · Gemma 3 Technical Report (Mar 2025)
🚀

Tokens that cross the space bar

Spaces do not define words ("New York"). SuperBPE lets tokens span whitespace: at a fixed 200K vocabulary it encodes text with up to 33% fewer tokens, and an 8B model trained with it gains 4.0 points on average over 30 tasks.

Liu et al., SuperBPE: Space Travel for Language Models (COLM 2025)
🧩

Tokenizer-free models are catching up

Instead of a fixed BPE vocabulary, models read raw bytes and learn their own grouping: BLT forms patches by next-byte entropy, Bolmo converts an existing Olmo 3 model to bytes, and ByteFlow learns segmentation by compression.

Meta BLT (Dec 2024) · Ai2 Bolmo (Dec 2025) · ByteFlow (Mar 2026)
⚡

Static embeddings are back — for speed

A lookup table plus averaging, like the baseline on slide 176, trained with modern objectives: Sentence Transformers' static models run 100–400× faster on CPU and keep at least 85% of the quality of transformer encoders.

Hugging Face blog, Train 400x faster Static Embedding Models (Jan 2025)
🌐

Shared concepts across languages, inside LLMs

Cross-lingual structure (slide 152) shows up inside large models too: interpretability work found language-independent features for concepts such as "small" and "opposite", and the shared fraction grows with model scale.

Anthropic, Tracing the thoughts of a large language model (Mar 2025)
